Challenges

The biggest challenge in producing the fashion film was creating a seamless transition from an urban city setting into a surreal “Impossible Reality” underwater world.

Two key issues emerged:

  • Limited Camera Control: Even after extensive prompting, Midjourney and RunwayML couldn’t achieve the precise camera movement needed for the transition.
  • Visual Inconsistency: Attempts to introduce the underwater world directly into the city scene felt unnatural and disrupted the film’s clean, minimalist aesthetic.
Solutions

Rather than spending more generation credits on unpredictable prompt iterations, I redesigned the transition around the edit:

  1. Controlled Fall: The protagonist falls backwards, creating a natural setup for the transition.
  2. Der „Hard Cut“: Just before impact, a hard cut maintains the momentum while hiding the change of environment.
  3. Scene Transition & Morph: The cut moves directly into a separately generated underwater sequence, where the protagonist floats weightlessly. A subtle morph then transitions into the final dialogue scene. The result is a cleaner transition that preserves the visual language of the film while working around the limitations of generative camera control.

Challenges

The campaign’s key visual features a stylized human head wearing sunglasses, fully encased inside a transparent, high-gloss resin block.

The main challenge was creating consistent variations of the concept, with different faces and eyewear, without breaking the complex material and lighting effects.

  • The Technical Challenge: The face and sunglasses needed to remain convincingly embedded inside the block, with realistic refraction, reflections, and surface depth.
  • Even with image references, Omni Reference, and inpainting, Midjourney couldn’t produce consistent results. It either distorted the geometry of the block or changed the eyewear too drastically.
Solutions

Rather than spending more time on prompt iterations in Midjourney, I switched the workflow to RunwayML Aleph 2.0 and used its strengths for controlled image editing:

  1. Targeted Element Replacement: Aleph 2.0 preserved the existing geometry, lighting, and material properties while allowing me to precisely replace and vary individual elements inside the block.

For efficient e-commerce asset creation, I built a custom Qwen Image Edit 2511 workflow in ComfyUI that combines up to three independent references, such as the model, product, and pose or background. Two integrated Edit Model Reference nodes feed clothing textures and structural details directly into the generation process, helping preserve realistic folds, shadows, lighting, and material behavior. The result is a reproducible workflow for photorealistic e-commerce imagery, with the added flexibility of running an open-weights model on private infrastructure for greater control over data and production.
